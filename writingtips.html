Writing Tips

Turn one idea into a story

Start small. Make each page matter.

Begin near the problem

Drop readers into action or an unusual moment rather than explaining everything first.

Give your character a want

Choose something they need in this scene. A want creates motion and gives your story a direction.

Use specific details

Describe one revealing object or action instead of a list of general facts.

Revise with purpose

Read once for the big story arc, then revise for pacing, dialogue, and clear language.

Simple idea, complete story

1. The simple idea

A student forgets their lunch at home.

2. Add a character

Jayla brings a lunch made to look like a tiny work of art.

3. Add a problem

At lunch, Jayla discovers an empty lunch bag—but the newest student is eating a dry granola bar.

4. Choose a change

Jayla has to decide whether to keep her perfect lunch or share it. She splits the bento and sits with the newest student.

Story opening: “The first thing Jayla noticed was that her lunch bag was light. The second thing was that Lin had no lunch at all.”

What judges look for

Original voice

Does the story sound like only you could have written it?

Clear craft

Do the sentences, pacing, and structure help the reader follow the story?

Theme connection

Does the story use the prompt in a fresh, meaningful way?
